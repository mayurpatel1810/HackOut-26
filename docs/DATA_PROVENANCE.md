## Roles

| Role | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `PRIMARY` | Methodologically and geographically appropriate. Used. |
| `REFERENCE` | Usable, but from another geography. **Used and flagged `REFERENCE ONLY`**, with reduced confidence and an explanation on the result. |
| `NOT_APPLICABLE` | Never used. Shown in the Evidence Passport with the reason. |

The full matrix is data, not code: `database/migrations/V6__seed_sources_and_policy.sql`,
generated by `scripts/gen_source_policy.py`. 142 rows across 13 categories × 4
geography buckets × 3 sources.

## India — the cases that matter

**Grid electricity → CEA, and only CEA.**
EPA eGRID describes US subregional grids. The UK 2026 *Overseas electricity*
sheet publishes **no factors at all** — it now only points at EEA/EPA/SEAI/RTE/
IEA. So CEA is not merely preferred for Indian electricity; within these three
datasets it is the only verified option.

Of CEA's five published series, EcoForge selects **Weighted Average Grid
Emission Rate (incl. RES, captive)** — 0.678033 kgCO₂/kWh for FY 2025-26. The
operating, build and combined margins are ingested and tagged `grid_marginal`,
because they answer a different question (what a new project displaces) and must
never substitute for a consumer's annual Scope 2 inventory.

**Fuel combustion → CEA.** Indian coal has a materially different calorific
value from US or UK coal. CEA publishes gCO₂/MJ on a gross-CV basis with the
oxidation factor applied. Per-mass and per-volume factors are **derived** inside
the parser from CEA's own GCV and density assumptions, each carrying its
derivation string.

> **The cross-check that validates the whole derivation chain.** CEA
> independently publishes an oil-specific value at `Assumptions!D71` =
> **2.88858 gCO₂/ml**. Our derived furnace-oil factor, reached through
> GCV × density × EF, is **2.88839 kgCO₂/litre** — a 0.0066 % deviation. This is
> asserted in `tests/test_ingestion.py`.

**Materials, waste, freight, travel, water, refrigerants → UK 2026, flagged
`REFERENCE ONLY`.** No Indian equivalent exists in the supplied datasets. The
flag matters most for materials: UK cradle-to-gate factors embed a UK grid that
is roughly five times less carbon intensive than India's, so the true Indian
figure is almost certainly higher. EcoForge says so rather than presenting the
number as Indian.

## The same activity, three sources

| Diesel combustion | Factor | Basis | Role for an Indian factory |
|---|---|---|---|
| CEA | 2.52132 kgCO₂/litre | gross CV, **CO₂ only**, Indian NCV/density | PRIMARY |
| EPA | 2.69720 kgCO₂e/litre | HHV, CO₂ only, US distillate No. 2 | REFERENCE |
| UK 2026 | 2.66155 kgCO₂e/litre | **CO₂e incl. CH₄/N₂O**, UK mineral diesel | REFERENCE |

A 7 % spread across three official publications, and it is *real*: different
fuel specifications, different calorific conventions, different gas coverage.
Averaging them produces a number that belongs to no methodology. EcoForge shows
all three side by side in the Evidence Passport and states which one was used.

## Gas coverage

CEA publishes **CO₂ only**. Every CEA-sourced result therefore carries the
limitation *"Gas coverage: CO₂ only … the result is not a complete CO₂e figure"*.
EPA and UK factors are combined to CO₂e using the GWP values read out of the EPA
workbook itself (CH₄ = 28, N₂O = 265, IPCC AR5) rather than hard-coded.

## Where the datasets run out

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Silica sand embodied carbon | **Verified data unavailable.** None of the three datasets publishes a factor. The demo factory's 310 t/yr of moulding sand is reported as unresolved, and coverage falls to 87.5 % accordingly. |
| Steel shot / abrasive media | Verified data unavailable. The abrasive-recovery intervention is surfaced with `NOT_QUANTIFIED` impact. |
| Peer benchmarks | These are emission-factor sources, not company benchmark databases. `benchmark_results` exists and is populated with `UNAVAILABLE` plus the reason. |
| Indian T&D losses | The UK T&D factor is `REFERENCE ONLY` and flagged as a poor proxy — India's losses are substantially higher. Replace it with a utility figure before external reporting. |

## Level 3 is kept separate on purpose

`data/curated/circular_interventions.json` holds 20 interventions with 27
evidence records. It is a **curated knowledge base, not an emission-factor
dataset**, and nothing in it is ever presented as if it came from CEA, EPA or
the UK Government. Every record carries `source_name`, `source_title`,
`source_url`, `publication_year`, `evidence_type`, `supports` and `confidence`.

16 of the 27 evidence records carry `verification_required: true`. These were
curated from domain knowledge and are surfaced in the UI as **"Source needs
verification"**. Check them against the primary document before any number
derived from them appears in an external report.

## Two guidance rules that constrain the product

**UK 2026 *Material use*:** emissions from procured material "may be …
apportioned by the required weights of each material". EcoForge therefore splits
a material line by stated recycled content across the published
primary-production and closed-loop factors — exactly the worked example in the
sheet — and takes **no avoided-burden credit**.

**UK 2026 *Waste disposal*:** these factors "cannot be used to determine the
relative lifecycle merit of different waste management options", because
recycling emissions are attributed to the *user* of the recovered material.
EcoForge therefore presents a diversion as a change in **reported Scope 3 waste
emissions under GHG Protocol attribution**, never as a lifecycle saving, and
never credits the waste producer with the avoided primary production.
